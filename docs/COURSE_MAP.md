# Course plan

This course starts with the basics and builds your understanding one step at a time. You do not need to know biology or chemistry before you begin.

Each module will include a lesson, useful reading, an example, practice questions, and a short task.

## How the practical work is taught

- **Computer practice:** You will use made-up names and DNA results. These exercises can be done on your computer.
- **Learning about lab methods:** You will learn what each laboratory step does and why it matters.
- **Real laboratory work:** Work with real samples and laboratory equipment must only happen in an approved teaching or testing laboratory, with trained supervision.

## Module 1: DNA and inheritance

Learn what DNA is, where it is found, what chromosomes and genes are, and how a child receives DNA from both biological parents.

**Practice:** Work through simple examples showing which DNA versions a child could inherit.

**You should be able to explain:** Why a child normally gets one copy of an autosomal DNA marker from each biological parent.

## Module 2: STR markers

Learn what STR markers are, why they are useful in paternity testing, and how scientists describe the different versions of a marker.

**Practice:** Read simple made-up STR results and identify the two versions shown for each person.

**You should be able to explain:** What an STR marker is and what an allele means.

## Module 3: Cheek swabs and sample records

Learn why cheek swabs are used, how identity and consent are checked, and why samples must be labeled and tracked carefully.

**Practice:** Fill out a sample form and tracking record using a made-up family.

**You should be able to explain:** Why every sample needs a clear label and a record of who handled it.

**Important:** This course does not ask you to collect or handle real people's samples.

## Module 4: Taking DNA out of cells

Learn how cells are opened and DNA is separated from other material. Learn why the amount and quality of DNA are checked.

**Practice:** Follow a simple diagram of the process and review made-up test results.

**You should be able to explain:** Why a laboratory checks DNA quality before moving to the next step.

Real laboratory work must follow the exact instructions for the approved test kit and the laboratory's procedures.

## Module 5: PCR

Learn how PCR makes many copies of selected DNA regions so they can be measured and read.

You will learn what DNA primers, enzymes, and controls do.

**Practice:** Label the main stages of PCR and answer questions about made-up test results.

**You should be able to explain:** Why PCR is used before reading STR markers.

## Module 6: Reading DNA fragments

Learn how a machine separates DNA fragments by size and detects them. This process is called capillary electrophoresis.

**Practice:** Read simple diagrams and made-up examples showing the signals produced by the machine.

**You should be able to explain:** How the machine helps a laboratory identify STR alleles.

## Module 7: Building a DNA profile

Learn how the results from different STR markers are put together to make a DNA profile. Learn why results must be checked for errors.

**Practice:** Read and organize made-up DNA profiles.

**You should be able to explain:** What information a DNA profile contains and why quality checks matter.

## Module 8: Comparing the family profiles

Learn how scientists compare the child's profile with the mother's and the alleged father's profiles.

**Practice:** Compare the results marker by marker using made-up examples.

**You should be able to explain:** The difference between a result that is consistent with a relationship and a result that rules out a proposed relationship.

## Module 9: Understanding the calculations

Learn how scientists use allele frequencies and probability calculations to measure how strongly the DNA results support one explanation over another.

**Practice:** Use a small made-up dataset to work through the calculations.

**You should be able to explain:** Why a DNA match is not the same thing as saying there is a 100% probability of paternity.

## Module 10: Writing the results

Learn what a clear paternity testing report contains, including the samples tested, methods used, results, calculations, and limits of the conclusion.

**Practice:** Write a sample report using the made-up results from the course.

**You should be able to explain:** What the results show, what they do not show, and why the report must be checked.

## Final practice case

At the end, you will work through one complete made-up case. You will review the sample records, look at the DNA profiles, compare the family members, complete the calculations, and write a practice report.

The case will use fictional people and fictional results. It will not represent a real test or a legal conclusion.
